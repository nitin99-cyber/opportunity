# OPPORTUNITY RADAR
## Product Requirements Document (PRD) & Complete Development Roadmap

**Version:** 1.0  
**Date:** 13 November 2026  
**Product:** Opportunity Radar  
**Initial market:** India  
**Product type:** Personalized internship and research-opportunity discovery engine  
**Status:** Initial prototype and source-registry milestone developed; automated discovery roadmap pending.

---

# 1. Product vision

Opportunity Radar is a personalized discovery engine that continuously searches the internet for internship and research opportunities across Indian Institutes of Technology (IITs), government research institutions, laboratories, departments and individual professors or scientists.

Its purpose is to solve a common problem: valuable opportunities are scattered across hundreds of institutional websites, department pages, PDFs, laboratory websites and individual researcher announcements. Students cannot manually monitor all these sources, and opportunities can close before students discover them.

Opportunity Radar will:

1. Discover opportunities from multiple public sources.
2. Identify internship announcements, research projects and informal research opportunities.
3. Extract deadlines, eligibility, funding, duration and application instructions.
4. Verify information using original sources.
5. Match opportunities against a student's academic profile and interests.
6. Rank relevant opportunities.
7. Track application deadlines and statuses.
8. Display newly discovered and relevant opportunities on a personalized dashboard.

**Core principle:** Discover broadly, verify carefully, rank intelligently and never fabricate an opportunity.

The engine must support:

| Category | Description |
|---|---|
| Summer internships | Summer research and technical internships |
| Winter internships | Winter research programmes |
| Semester internships | Research during an academic semester |
| Research internships | Faculty or laboratory research positions |
| Project assistant | Paid or unpaid research project roles |

# 5. Product principles

1. Official sources take priority over secondary sources.
2. Every opportunity must retain its source URL.
3. A researcher working in a field does not automatically mean they accept interns.
4. Potential openings must not be presented as confirmed vacancies.
5. Eligibility must be evaluated separately from relevance.
6. Old announcements must be identified as stale.
7. Conflicting information must be flagged.
8. Users must control their own applications and professor communications.
9. Notifications will initially be dashboard-only.
10. Public sources must be accessed responsibly.

# 6. Functional requirements

## FR-01: Candidate profile

Users must be able to maintain an editable profile.

Fields:

- Name or display name (optional)
- Institution
- Degree
- Branch
- Current year of study
- Graduation year
- CGPA (optional)
- Skills
- Programming languages
- Technical interests
- Research interests
- Preferred locations
- Funding preference
- Preferred internship duration
- Availability dates

The matching engine must use this profile.

## FR-02: Source registry

Maintain a structured list of approved and pending information sources.

Every source should store:

- Institution
- Source name
- Source URL
- Source category
- Official/secondary trust level
- Allowed domain
- Relevant path prefix
- Review status
- Monitoring frequency
- Last checked time
- Last successful check
- Next scheduled check
- Failure count

Source states:

- PENDING_REVIEW
- APPROVED
- PAUSED
- REJECTED

A homepage must not automatically be treated as an internship announcement page.

## FR-03: Opportunity discovery

The system must find opportunities through:

1. Known official sources.
2. Search engine discovery.
3. RSS and Atom feeds.
4. Department pages.
5. Faculty directories.
6. Laboratory websites.
7. Project announcements.
8. Public PDF notices.
9. Public researcher announcements.

## FR-04: Researcher discovery

Find researchers through official faculty directories and laboratory pages.

Store:

- Researcher name
- Institution
- Department
- Designation
- Research areas
- Official profile URL
- Laboratory URL
- Public professional contact information, where explicitly published
- Related announcements
- Evidence of recruitment signals
- Last verified date

Do not infer that a researcher is recruiting solely from their research interests.

## FR-05: Opportunity extraction

Extract:

- Title
- Institution
- Department
- Laboratory
- Opportunity type
- Description
- Eligibility
- Degree requirements
- Branch requirements
- Year-of-study requirements
- CGPA requirements
- Skills
- Duration
- Start date
- Deadline
- Funding
- Stipend amount
- Location
- Application procedure
- Application URL
- Contact information
- Original source URL
- Publication date
- Last verified date

Unknown information must remain UNKNOWN, not be guessed.

## FR-06: Verification

Each opportunity must have two independent classifications.

**Application status:**

- UPCOMING
- OPEN
- CLOSED
- PAUSED
- CANCELLED
- UNKNOWN

**Verification status:**

- VERIFIED
- PARTIALLY_VERIFIED
- UNVERIFIED
- STALE
- CONFLICTED

A verified opening must have credible supporting evidence from an original or otherwise authoritative source.

## FR-07: Eligibility engine

Eligibility states:

- ELIGIBLE
- POSSIBLY_ELIGIBLE
- INELIGIBLE
- INSUFFICIENT_INFORMATION

Eligibility checks:

- Degree
- Branch
- Year of study
- Graduation year
- CGPA
- Skills
- Nationality or other explicitly stated requirements
- Other programme-specific criteria

Never label a student eligible when a mandatory condition is unknown.

## FR-08: Opportunity matching

Each opportunity receives a relevance score from 0–100.

Initial proposed weighting:

| Factor | Weight |
|---|---:|
| Research-interest relevance | 30% |
| Skill alignment | 25% |
| Academic suitability | 15% |
| Opportunity quality and evidence | 10% |
| Funding preference | 10% |
| Recency | 10% |

Eligibility remains a separate classification and cannot be replaced by the relevance score.

The system should explain why each opportunity was recommended.

## FR-09: Dashboard

The dashboard must display:

- Newly discovered opportunities
- Open opportunities
- Upcoming deadlines
- Saved opportunities
- Best profile matches
- Potential researcher leads
- Recently discovered researchers
- Source health
- Unverified information requiring review

Filters:

- Institution
- Organization type
- Department
- Research area
- Opportunity category
- Funding
- Deadline
- Eligibility
- Verification status
- Relevance score

## FR-10: Application tracking

Users can manually track:

- Interested
- Saved
- Preparing application
- Applied
- Interview
- Accepted
- Rejected
- Withdrawn

Store application dates, notes, links and follow-up dates.

The engine must not automatically submit applications or contact professors.

## FR-11: Reminders

Dashboard reminders for:

- Application deadlines
- Upcoming opportunities
- Saved opportunities nearing closure
- Follow-up dates
- Sources requiring review

External notifications are outside the initial release.

# 7. Complete development roadmap

The following phases define the complete intended product. A phase is complete only after its acceptance criteria have been met.

## Phase 0 — Project foundation

**Goal:** Establish a working application.

Tasks:

- Create Node.js project.
- Configure Express.
- Create HTML/CSS/JavaScript frontend.
- Configure SQLite.
- Build basic dashboard.
- Create opportunity records.
- Create researcher records.
- Create profile management.
- Create source registration.

Deliverables:

- Working local application.
- Database.
- Basic REST API.
- Initial documentation.

Acceptance criteria:

- Application starts locally.
- Records can be created and retrieved.
- Profile can be edited.
- Data persists after restarting the server.

**Status:** Initial starter implemented.

## Phase 1 — Institutional source registry

**Goal:** Build the institution and source foundation.

Tasks:

- Add all 23 IITs.
- Add initial government research organizations.
- Add source categories.
- Add source trust levels.
- Add approval workflow.
- Add pause and reject controls.
- Add monitoring frequency fields.
- Add source health metadata.
- Allow registration of exact faculty, department, lab and internship pages.

Deliverables:

- Seed script.
- Source registry UI.
- Source-management APIs.
- SQLite schema migration.

Acceptance criteria:

- Institutions can be seeded without duplicate URLs.
- Sources can be searched and filtered.
- Sources can be approved, rejected or paused.
- Pending homepages are never treated as confirmed opportunity pages.

**Status:** Implemented in the Phase 2 project ZIP; not yet pushed to GitHub.

## Phase 2 — Discovery infrastructure

**Goal:** Make the system capable of finding new public information.

Tasks:

1. Select a search provider.
2. Build a search-provider adapter.
3. Create institution-specific search queries.
4. Create department-specific search queries.
5. Create researcher-specific search queries.
6. Add RSS/Atom discovery.
7. Add public-page fetching.
8. Add PDF discovery.
9. Store discovered URLs.
10. Remove duplicate URLs.
11. Track discovery runs.

Example queries:

- "undergraduate research internship IIT computer science"
- "summer research intern IIT cybersecurity"
- "project assistant computer science DRDO"
- "research internship CSIR computer science"
- "IIT faculty cybersecurity research group"
- "undergraduate students research project AI IIT"

Acceptance criteria:

- Discovery can run manually.
- Results are stored with their source and discovery time.
- Duplicate URLs are not repeatedly inserted.
- Failed sources are recorded.
- Search results are not automatically labelled verified openings.

## Phase 3 — Website monitoring and content extraction

**Goal:** Extract useful information from public pages.

Tasks:

- Fetch approved pages.
- Detect page changes.
- Parse HTML.
- Parse RSS and Atom.
- Extract links.
- Extract PDF notices.
- Extract publication dates.
- Store page snapshots.
- Detect expired announcements.
- Identify changed deadlines.
- Track source failures.

Technical requirements:

- Request timeouts.
- Per-domain rate limits.
- Domain allowlists.
- URL validation.
- SSRF protection.
- Response-size limits.
- Content-type validation.
- Robots and site-terms compliance.
- Clear user-agent identification.

Acceptance criteria:

- Only approved sources are fetched.
- A failed source does not stop other monitoring.
- Page snapshots and extracted content can be traced to the original URL.
- Changed pages can be identified.

## Phase 4 — AI opportunity extraction

**Goal:** Turn raw content into structured opportunity records.

Use Gemini API initially through a provider adapter.

Tasks:

- Detect whether a page contains an opportunity.
- Extract structured fields.
- Extract eligibility criteria.
- Extract funding information.
- Extract deadlines.
- Extract duration.
- Extract application procedures.
- Identify the relevant institution and department.
- Link every extracted field to supporting evidence where possible.
- Store extraction confidence.
- Identify missing information.

AI output must use a strict structured schema.

Important:

- AI must not invent dates, stipend amounts or eligibility.
- Uncertain fields must be UNKNOWN.
- Low-confidence extractions should enter a review queue.
- Original content must remain available for verification.

Acceptance criteria:

- Structured opportunities can be created from supported pages.
- Each extracted record retains its original URL.
- Unknown values remain unknown.
- Extraction failures are logged and retryable.

## Phase 5 — Opportunity verification engine

**Goal:** Determine whether an opportunity is real, current and actionable.

Tasks:

- Verify source authority.
- Verify opening status.
- Check publication and deadline dates.
- Detect expired announcements.
- Detect duplicate announcements.
- Detect conflicting dates.
- Identify outdated PDFs.
- Check application links.
- Record verification history.
- Add human-review workflow.

Acceptance criteria:

- Application status and verification status are independent.
- Conflicting sources produce CONFLICTED.
- Old information can become STALE.
- No unverified lead is presented as a confirmed vacancy.

## Phase 6 — Researcher and laboratory discovery

**Goal:** Build a searchable database of potential research supervisors and laboratories.

Tasks:

- Discover IIT faculty directories.
- Discover department staff pages.
- Discover government laboratory scientists.
- Extract public professional profiles.
- Identify research areas.
- Identify laboratory affiliations.
- Identify official profile links.
- Associate researchers with related opportunities.
- Identify explicit recruitment announcements.
- Track potential openings and informal public signals.

Researcher signal classifications:

- CONFIRMED_OPEN
- POTENTIAL_OPENING
- RESEARCH_LEAD
- CLOSED
- UNKNOWN

Acceptance criteria:

- Researcher profiles are traceable to public professional sources.
- Research interests do not automatically create confirmed openings.
- Recruitment evidence is stored separately.
- Users can search researchers by institution and research area.

## Phase 7 — Personalized eligibility and matching engine

**Goal:** Identify opportunities most suitable for the student's profile.

Tasks:

- Normalize skills and research interests.
- Compare opportunity requirements with profile data.
- Calculate relevance score.
- Evaluate eligibility separately.
- Prioritize paid opportunities.
- Preserve valuable unpaid research.
- Explain ranking factors.
- Allow user-controlled preferences.

Acceptance criteria:

- Each opportunity has an explainable match score.
- Eligibility is independently classified.
- Missing mandatory criteria result in INSUFFICIENT_INFORMATION or POSSIBLY_ELIGIBLE as appropriate.
- Rankings can be filtered and sorted.

## Phase 8 — Dashboard and search experience

**Goal:** Make discovery results useful and easy to act on.

Tasks:

- Add saved opportunities.

# 8. Technical architecture

## Agreed technology stack

| Layer | Technology |
|---|---|
| Frontend | HTML |
| Styling | CSS |
| Frontend logic | Browser JavaScript |
| Backend | Node.js |
| API | Express |
| Database | SQLite |
| AI extraction | Gemini API |
| Discovery | Search API, RSS and public HTML |
| PDF extraction | Lightweight Node.js PDF parser |

Do not introduce React, TypeScript, microservices, Redis, Kafka or a complex cloud architecture unless a real requirement emerges.

# 11. Non-functional requirements

### Reliability

- One failed source must not stop the complete discovery process.
- Jobs must be retryable.
- Database records must remain consistent.
- Duplicate discovery must be controlled.

### Performance

- Paginate large result sets.
- Index common search fields.
- Keep HTML fetching lightweight.
- Avoid heavy browser automation as the default approach.

### Security

- Store API keys in environment variables.
- Never commit secrets to GitHub.
- Restrict source fetching to approved public domains.
- Block localhost, private IP ranges and unsafe redirects.
- Do not bypass authentication, CAPTCHAs or paywalls.

### Privacy

- Keep candidate data local during the initial release.
- Collect only information required for matching.
- Do not expose personal profile information to public sources.
- Do not automatically send emails or messages.

### Legal and ethical requirements

- Respect robots directives, website terms and applicable law.
- Follow reasonable request rates.
- Prefer official institutional sources.
- Use public professional researcher information.
- Do not scrape protected or restricted content.

# 14. Definition of the finished product

Opportunity Radar will be considered ready for its first full release when:

1. It monitors approved sources across all 23 IITs and the initial government research organizations.
2. It can discover opportunities from official pages, search results, RSS feeds and public documents.
3. It can discover public faculty and scientist profiles.
4. It distinguishes confirmed openings from potential research leads.
5. It extracts deadlines, eligibility, duration, funding and application instructions.
6. It preserves evidence for extracted information.
7. It detects stale, closed and conflicting announcements.
8. It evaluates student eligibility.
9. It ranks opportunities according to the candidate's profile.
10. It displays useful results in the dashboard.
11. It tracks saved opportunities and applications.
12. It automatically monitors approved sources on a schedule.
13. It provides reliable dashboard deadline reminders.

**The final product is not simply a website containing internship links. It is a personalized, evidence-backed research and internship intelligence engine that discovers opportunities students would otherwise miss.**
